HOW TO READ THIS TOOL
What this is. Research prototype running on synthetic crew data. Not cleared or approved by the FDA, and not for diagnosis or treatment. It is meant to be used under flight-surgeon oversight: it tells you what to raise, not what you have.
Four things are worth knowing before anything else.

It judges against three frames, not one. Every reading is compared with this crewmember before flight, with their own rolling norm in the current context, and with a published standard. The same heart rate can be normal in one frame and an alert in another, which is why the frame is always named.

It says when it does not know. Where no verified threshold exists, none is applied. Body mass and suit CO₂ are reported as trends with no band at all, rather than given an invented limit.

Two colour systems, deliberately. Red, amber and green say how far a reading sits from its band. NOW, WITHIN HOURS, TODAY and NOTE IT say how soon you must move. Nothing in the urgency scale is green, because green means in range and an action point never is.

Colour is never alone. Every level also carries a word and a distinct shape, so the screen still works for a colour-blind crewmember, in monochrome print, and under coloured cabin lighting.
NASA STANDARD MEASURES · WHAT THIS BUILD DRAWS ON
Five areas this build draws on.
NASA collects a defined set of measures across every mission so crew data can be compared between flights. Most of it needs a laboratory, a scanner or a centrifuge. Only the areas SYNAPSE takes at least one measurement in are listed.
WHY THERE ARE NO AGE OR SEX BANDS
No threshold in this build changes with a crewmember’s age or sex. That is a choice, not an omission. Almost everything here is judged against that person’s own preflight baseline and their own adapted range in flight, and a personal baseline already carries whatever their age, sex, build and fitness contribute — more faithfully than a population band fitted to people who are not them. Published limits are applied only where the limit is itself stated for everyone, as the career dose limit in NASA-STD-3001 [V1 4030] is.

The cost of this choice is stated plainly: where a reference range in the literature genuinely does differ by age or sex, this build does not model that difference, and the band it shows is the general adult one. Where no verified threshold exists at all, no band is applied and the reading is reported as a trend against that crewmember alone.
